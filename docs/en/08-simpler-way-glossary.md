# A word for testing a trading rule: the simpler way

_A trading rule has not earned anything until it beats simpler ways of trading the same stretch of history. What a simpler way is, and why it matters._

A rule can pass every test of luck and still not be worth having. The last question is plainer: does it do better than something simple?

## What it means

A simpler way is a way of trading the same market, over the same stretch of history, with almost no thinking in it: buy and hold, for example, or a plain rule that anyone could write in a line. It is not chosen to be good. It is chosen to be obvious, so that a rule has something fair to beat.

## Why it matters

A complicated rule that scores well on a stretch of history may only be riding what the market did anyway. If prices rose all year, many rules that bought look clever. If a plain way of trading the same stretch does as well, the complicated rule has added nothing but complication, and complication has costs: more trades, more settings to go wrong, more chances that the good score was fitted to the past.

## How to use it

Score the simpler ways on exactly the same history as the rule, with the same costs and the same scoring. Do it on history the rule never saw, not only on the history it was chosen from. Then look at the gap, not the rule's score alone. A rule well clear of every simpler way has earned a second look. A rule that only matches them has shown nothing yet, however good its own number looks.

## Where it sits among the other checks

It comes last. First, how good does "nothing" look: a run with no information in it. Then, history the rule never saw. Then, simpler ways of trading that same unseen stretch.
