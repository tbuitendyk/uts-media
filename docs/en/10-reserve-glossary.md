# A word for testing a trading rule: reserve

_Reserve is the last stretch of history, opened once, after every choice is made. What it is, why one look is all it gets, and how to keep it honest._

Most tests of a trading rule can be run again. This one cannot, and that is the whole of its value.

## What it means

Reserve is a stretch of history set aside before any work begins and opened last of all, after every setting has been chosen and every other check is done. Nothing about the rule is decided while it is closed. When it is opened, the rule meets history no choice was ever made on.

## Why it matters

Every time you look at a stretch of history and then change something, that stretch starts to shape the rule. Do it often enough and even a held-out stretch becomes part of the fitting, quietly. Reserve guards against that by being looked at once. A rule that only did well on history you kept returning to may have learned that history; a rule that holds up on reserve has been judged on something it could not have learned.

## How to use it

Decide where reserve sits before you start, and do not look inside it. Make every choice on the other parts of history. When nothing is left to adjust, open it once and write down what you see. If the rule fails there, the honest move is to drop it, not to tune it and open reserve again: a second look turns reserve into one more stretch you fitted to.

## Where it sits among the other checks

It is the last of the four parts of history: train, test, held, then reserve. Before it come the planted pattern, how good "nothing" looks, and the simpler ways of trading the same stretch.
