# A word for testing a trading rule: the greenlight

_A greenlight freezes a rule that passed: its settings, the evidence behind it and the software release it was proven on. What that means and why a result needs it._

A result you cannot reproduce is a story, not evidence. The greenlight is how a rule that passed keeps its evidence with it.

## What it means

A greenlight is a fixed snapshot of a rule that has passed its checks. It holds three things together: the rule's exact settings, the record sets behind the result, and the release of the software it was proven on. Once made, it does not change.

## Why it matters

Rules drift. A setting gets nudged, the software is updated, the history is refreshed, and a month later nobody can say which version earned the good result. Without a fixed record, the rule running today may not be the rule that was tested. A greenlight closes that gap: what goes forward is exactly what was proven, and anyone can trace it back.

## How to use it

Freeze a rule only after it has passed every check, not before. Keep the settings, the evidence and the software version together, never one without the others. When something changes, make a new snapshot and test it again; do not edit the old one. Then run forward only what a snapshot names.

## Where it sits among the other checks

It comes after them all: the planted pattern, how good "nothing" looks, history the rule never saw (held, then reserve), and the simpler ways of trading the same stretch. A greenlight is what a rule earns by passing, and what paper trading then runs.
